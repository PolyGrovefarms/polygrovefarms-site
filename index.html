🌿 Our Story & Homestead Mission

Located in the lush rainforest community of Fern Forest, Hawaii, Polygrove Farms is an integrated homestead and farm dedicated to sustainable agriculture, ethical pasture management, and thriving apiaries.

We strictly utilize Korean Natural Farming (KNF) principles to nurture our high-elevation soil and crops, bypassing chemical inputs in favor of indigenous microorganisms and nutrient-dense, natural amendments. Our diverse pasture-raised flock includes chickens, Runner ducks, heritage turkeys, and alert guinea fowl—all thriving in the crisp Big Island rainforest air alongside our aquaculture and nursery crops.

Alongside our fresh produce and poultry, we tend honeybee colonies under our artisan brand, Hilo Hive & Flame, offering raw island honey, hand-poured beeswax candles, and natural body care, while serving our neighbors with humane bee swarm relocation.

🐝 Natural Hive Care & Swarm Protection

At Hilo Hive & Flame, our bees are managed with chemical-free, natural beekeeping standards. Every jar of honey and block of beeswax comes from colonies foraging freely across native Big Island rainforest flora. When wild swarms appear, we safely rescue and rehome them in our apiary.

Chickens

Blue eggs

Ameraucana

The blue egg layer

If you've dreamed of opening the nesting box to find sky-blue eggs, this is your bird. Calm, friendly, and quiet, Ameraucanas are wonderful for families with keiki.

More about this breed

Developed in the United States in the 1970s to keep the famous blue-egg trait while producing a healthier, sturdier chicken, the Ameraucana earned official breed recognition in 1984. Look for their fluffy cheek muffs and small pea combs. Chicks hatch in a mix of colors, so every one is a little surprise.

Brown eggs

Rhode Island Red

The classic American farm hen

One of the most famous chickens in the world: a dependable brown egg layer, hardy enough to handle just about anything. A perfect first-flock bird.

More about this breed

Developed in New England in the late 1800s as a dual-purpose bird, the Rhode Island Red often lays 5 or more eggs a week. With deep mahogany-red feathers, Reds are as pretty as they are practical: confident, easygoing foragers that love to roam the yard.

Brown eggs

Red Star

The egg-laying champion

Bred for one thing above all: eggs, and lots of them. These gentle, hardworking hens can lay close to 300 brown eggs a year.

More about this breed

Red Stars often start laying earlier than other breeds and keep going steadily. They're low-maintenance and easy to handle, perfect for beginners and busy households alike, and their golden-red feathers glow in the Hawaiian sun.

$10 each, straight run

Large brown eggs

Jersey Giant

The gentle giant

The largest American chicken breed. Grown roosters can reach around 13 pounds, yet Jersey Giants are calm and gentle with people.

More about this breed

Developed in New Jersey in the late 1800s, Jersey Giants come in black and white varieties. They lay large brown eggs and make excellent meat birds too. They take a little longer to grow up than other breeds, but a Jersey Giant strutting across the yard is worth the wait.

White eggs

Andalusian

The blue beauty from Spain

Slate-blue feathers edged in darker lacing make this one of the most striking chickens you'll ever see. A warm-climate breed that's a natural fit for Hawaii.

More about this breed

Originally from the Andalusia region of Spain, Andalusians are lively, alert foragers. The hens lay large white eggs and rarely go broody, so they keep on laying. Because of how the blue color is inherited, chicks can hatch blue, black, or a speckled white called "splash," so every hatch is a little different.

Heritage Turkeys

Bourbon Red

The heritage beauty

Rich chestnut-red feathers with snowy white wing and tail tips. Calm around people, great foragers, and a perfect first turkey for any homestead.

More about this breed

Developed in Bourbon County, Kentucky, in the late 1800s, the Bourbon Red is one of America's most treasured heritage turkeys, known for flavorful meat. Turkey poults are hard to find in Hawaii, since mainland hatcheries can't ship them here.

$15 each, unsexed
Pay now

Narragansett

A rare piece of American history

Striking steel gray, black, tan, and white feathers. Calm, good-natured, and wonderful mothers. Raising them helps keep a rare breed alive.

More about this breed

Named after Narragansett Bay in Rhode Island, this heritage breed dates back to the 1800s and was once a New England farm favorite. Today it's a rare breed, which makes these poults a special find on the Big Island.
